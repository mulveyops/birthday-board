## 1. St Hedwig's Church — block 11
*1702 N Humboldt Ave · 48 × 26 m*

Map: `place_of_worship`, cream city brick, Romanesque Revival, architect Henry
Messmer, parish founded 1871, `building:levels=6`, historic.

**Draft description:** Saint Hedwig Catholic Church, the visual crest of Brady
Street. A **cream city brick** body — pale warm cream, never red — with stone
trim and round-arched Romanesque windows. One tall central tower carrying a
**copper-patina-green spire**, the tallest thing for blocks around, with a
slightly bulbous Eastern-European swell where the spire meets the tower. Long
axis east–west: tower and main doors at the WEST end facing Humboldt, the tall
gabled nave running back east. Slate roof, dark wood doors, blue-purple
stained glass.

## 2. Glorioso's Italian Market — block 20
*1011 E Brady St · 40 × 30 m*

Map: `shop=supermarket`, Italian, stucco, 2 levels, architect Myers E.
Becongia, 1913, historic — **`name:historic=Astor Theatre`**, and it was the
Brady Street Pharmacy until 2010.

**Draft description:** An old movie house turned Italian grocery. Wide, low
theatre massing with a tall flat parapet, clearly bigger and flatter-topped
than its neighbours. Light stucco body, a long run of storefront glass along
Brady, a bold horizontal **GLORIOSO'S** sign band, and Italian tricolour —
green, white, red — in the awnings. The biggest commercial mass on the street.

## 3. Wolski's Tavern — block 4
*1836 N Pulaski St · 18 × 13 m*

Map: bar, **wood**, front-gabled, 2 levels + basement, 2 units, 1895,
historic, outdoor seating.

**Draft description:** A house that became a tavern, and the most beloved dive
in the neighbourhood. Front-gabled two-storey **wood** building with light
clapboard siding, dark roof, domestic proportions — small where everything
near it is brick. A painted **WOLSKI'S** signboard band across the first
floor, warm amber windows, a couple of sidewalk picnic tables. Its charm is
its modesty; keep it characterful rather than grand.

## 4. Pulaski Street Playfield — block 4
*1840 N Pulaski St · park, 170 × 86 m · **shares block 4 with Wolski's***

Corrected from Steven's aerial photo — our OSM extract was incomplete, it had
only the tennis courts and playground and **missed the softball diamond**.

What is actually there, west to east across the block:
- **Pulaski Playground** at the north-west corner: play structures on a pale
  sand/wood-chip surface, paths curling through it
- a **hard court** immediately east of it — deep red/maroon surface with white
  painted lines, the brightest colour on the block
- a wide **open green lawn** through the middle
- **Pulaski Softball Field** filling the east end: a big tan skinned infield
  with its diamond and backstop, grass outfield around it
- mature trees along the edges, heaviest along the south and east
- **Wolski's Tavern sits at the south-west corner of this same block**, on
  Pulaski Street, with the park wrapping around behind and beside it

**Draft description:** The neighbourhood's green lung, a game challenge site,
and the one block on the board where the eye rests. Read left to right: a
colourful **playground** on pale sand at the north-west, a **red hard court**
with white lines beside it, a broad **open lawn**, and a **softball diamond**
at the east end — tan infield, backstop, grass outfield. Paths wander through,
mature shade trees line the edges, benches and a picnic table. Openness is the
point: no buildings inside the park, and the green should read as generous and
public from across the board.

## 5. Fink's — block 1
*1875 N Humboldt Ave · 18 × 7 m*

Map: bar, `building=retail`, front-gabled, **single storey**, built 1894,
remodelled 2001 by Scott Genke. A wonderful lineage in the tags: Listwan's
(1934) → Al Hauke's (1946) → D's on Humboldt → Jacqueline's → Baldy's → Mama
Roux → Red Room (2001) → Fink's (2012).

**Draft description:** A small, single-storey front-gabled corner bar — long
and narrow, sitting right up on the sidewalk. Modest and neighbourly rather
than grand, with a painted **FINK'S** sign board across the gable front, warm
lit windows and a door on the corner. The kind of tavern that has been six
other bars and still looks like all of them.

## 6. Red Lion Pub — block 1
*1850 N Water St · 43 × 11 m*

Map: `amenity=pub`, British, **brick**, commercial vernacular, 2 levels +
basement, retail + apartments, 1890 — **`name:1911=Gettleman Brewery`**, and
"on Tannery Row".

**Draft description:** A long two-storey **brick** commercial block — by far
the widest building on its block at 43 m — with shopfront below and apartments
above. Nineteenth-century brewery-era masonry: flat roofline, regular window
rhythm, stone sills. A British pub at street level: dark painted shopfront, a
hanging **RED LION** sign, warm windows, a few pavement tables.

## 7. Eagle Park Brewing — block 7
*823 E Hamilton St · 30 × 20 m*

Map: `craft=brewery`, `microbrewery=yes`, **`building=garage`**, Mediterranean
Revival, brick, single storey + basement, industrial + retail use, 1920 —
**`name:1920=Gallun Tanneries Garage`**, outdoor seating.

**Draft description:** A 1920s tannery garage turned brewery taproom. Low,
wide, single-storey **brick** industrial building with Mediterranean Revival
touches — a shaped parapet, arched openings, tile accents. Big garage-door
bays along the front, opened up with glass. An outdoor patio with picnic
benches and string lights. Industrial bones, friendly use.

## 8. The Hi Hat — block 13
*Hi Hat Lounge, 1701 N Arlington Pl (22 × 15 m) + Hi Hat Garage, 1709 (22 × 10 m)*

**One venue in two adjoining buildings** — paint them together as a single
landmark, roughly 22 × 25 m over both.

Map: the **Lounge** is cream city brick, two storeys, three units, bar since
1998. The **Garage** next door is `building=garage`, concrete, single storey,
1922, historic — **`name:historic=Zawatski Garage`**. Both have outdoor
seating.

**Draft description:** One bar living in two buildings side by side, and the
contrast between them is the whole picture. On the corner, the **Hi Hat
Lounge**: a two-storey **cream city brick** building with apartment windows
above and a dark painted bar front below. Attached to it, the **Hi Hat
Garage**: a low single-storey **concrete** garage from 1922 that kept every
bit of the garage about it — wide door openings glazed and thrown open to the
street, flat roofline, plain parapet. A **HI HAT** sign band, sidewalk tables
under awnings, warm light spilling out of both. Tall and squat together.

## 9. Hosed on Brady — block 22
*1689 N Franklin Pl · 18 × 10 m*

Map: bar, **brick**, `building:architecture=boomtown`, 2 levels + basement,
current bar 2009 — previously Ziegler Brewing (1938), G+G Bar, Schlitz
Krueger's (1954), Franklin Place (1979–2009).

**Draft description:** A two-storey **brick** corner tavern with a
**boomtown false front** — a flat, raised parapet squaring off the roof and
making the building look taller than it is, which is its signature. Corner
entrance, warm lit windows, a painted sign band. A century of beer-hall
history in a small footprint.

## 10. Y-Not II — block 28
*706 E Lyon St · 24 × 21 m*

Map: bar since **1968**, in a **three-storey brick apartment building** —
Spanish Colonial, 16 units, retail + apartments, architects Backes; Phaller.

**Draft description:** A dark, beloved corner dive on the ground floor of a
handsome **three-storey Spanish Colonial brick apartment building** — the tall
one on its corner, with a low-pitched tile-look roof, arched detailing and
balconies above. The bar occupies the corner at street level: painted
shopfront, neon in the window, a small **Y-NOT II** sign. Tall building,
small bar.

## 11. Our house — 811 E Pleasant St — block 29
*16 × 10 m*

Map + Wisconsin Historical Society (HI104327): **Queen Anne, 1888**,
**cream city brick**, two-flat, 2 levels + basement, architect James Douglas,
built by Neff & Grather, historic name *Charles Walsh Residence*. WHS calls it
"a good example of Victorian residence constructed of Cream City Brick".

**Draft description:** An 1888 Queen Anne **cream city brick** two-flat — the
same pale warm brick as the church, in miniature, and the brick is what makes
it recognisable. Two storeys over a raised basement, front steps up to the
entrance, tall narrow Victorian windows with stone sills, a steep roof with
decorative gable trim. This is the party house: it should feel warm and
welcoming, the one home on the board you can tell is loved — lit windows,
flowers, something festive at the door.

## 12. Thomas house — 1680 N Cass St — block 19
*17 × 8 m*

Map + WHS (HI105820): **Queen Anne, 1890**, **asphalt** siding, two units,
one roof level.

**Draft description:** A **blue** 1890 Queen Anne two-flat, narrow and deep on
its lot — the classic Milwaukee proportion. Painted blue siding over asphalt
shingle (a genuinely Milwaukee material, not a mistake), a front porch with
steps up, tall narrow windows, a pitched roof with a modest decorative gable.
The blue is what identifies it: it should be the one clearly blue house on its
block. A homelier cousin to the cream-brick house on Pleasant.
